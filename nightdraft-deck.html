Slide 3 · Objectives & Customer Needs

A sales tool for the most valuable email a restaurant receives all week.

Hospitality has automated the easy bookings and ignored the hard ones. NightDraft is the first tool built for the segment OpenTable abandoned.

Every elite restaurant in London uses OpenTable, SevenRooms, Resy or Tock for tables of 1-6. Fully automated. Customer self-serves, restaurant gets the data, no human needed.

But for the bookings that actually move the P&L - takeovers, private dining, 8+ parties, corporate events, tasting menus - the same restaurants put "For larger bookings, please email us" on their website. The biggest transactions of the week get routed back to a slow, manual, error-prone email process.

A great first reply converts. A slow, generic, or under-priced one loses the booking.

What NightDraft does
1

Reads every inbound high-stakes inquiry

Within seconds of arrival.

2

Researches the guest lightly

Via public sources (LinkedIn role, company, online presence) to surface context the host would have looked up anyway, faster. Soft signal: "this guest works at a private equity firm, lean into the wine pairing menu."

3

Codifies the owner's playbook

Table-of-10 rules, takeover rules, voice, seasonality, exceptions - all into a private rules engine.

4

Drafts a tailored first reply into Gmail drafts

Same product, different reply for a GBP 2,000 takeover vs a GBP 400 birthday. Junior staff can deploy with the owner's voice when the owner can't.

The AI/ML edge - two compounding loops, both newly possible in 2025

Learning loop

Every owner edit before sending updates the playbook. The same mistake never happens twice.

Guest intelligence loop

An agent (Playwright-style web automation) researches every inbound inquiry. Builds a private guest-context layer that sharpens with every inquiry.

Why this is only possible now

Per-guest research at indie-restaurant economics is genuinely new. Two years ago it was impossible:

  • Pre-LLM: required a human concierge (~GBP 600/mo) or custom NLP pipeline (GBP 50k+ build).
  • Pre-frontier-models: synthesis ("this guest is a PE partner, lean into wine pairing") was unreliable.
  • Pre-vibe-coding: agent build would have taken a 5-person engineering team.
For the first time in hospitality history, a Bib Gourmand restaurant can have per-guest research running automatically on every high-stakes inquiry, at GBP 179/mo. What Michelin three-star restaurants pay full-time concierge teams to deliver, NightDraft brings to the indie segment. After 6 months your NightDraft is uniquely yours - a compounding moat ChatGPT and Claude cannot replicate.

Slide 8 · Consumer Choice
Restaurant operators are not enterprise buyers. They are owner-operators in the weeds. There is no RFI, no procurement, no committee.
The sale closes when two filters pass: "Does someone I trust already use it?" and "Am I in control of it?"
The decision frame

Filter 1 Trust signal

"Does someone I respect already use this?" Peer reference is the single highest-impact signal. Founder credibility is part of this filter. This is why door-to-door selling still works in restaurants - operators are used to enthusiastic founders walking in between services (10-11am or 3-4pm windows).

Filter 2 Control

"Am I in charge, or is the software making decisions for me?" Hard rejection of autosend. NightDraft embeds in the owner's existing workflow. Draft lands in Gmail drafts. Owner edits and sends. Nothing happens without the owner's hand on it. Filter 2 also absorbs "tech stack" concerns.

  • Single-conversation close is realistic when the demo lands.

Slide 12 · Perceptual Maps

NightDraft sits alone in the strong quadrant on both maps.

A perceptual map is a picture of how customers actually arrange competing options in their head. It exposes whitespace (empty quadrants), competitor vulnerability, and whether your differentiation is genuinely perceived. We built two maps because demand fit and competitive defence are different questions.

How to read a perceptual map.
  1. Two axes = the two attributes that drive the customer's choice.
  2. Each dot = a player in the market (you and your competitors).
  3. Empty quadrants = whitespace; clusters = commoditisation; stars = "ideal points" - where each customer segment wishes the perfect product sat.
Map 1 · Demand fit · what customers want
AI/ML capability × Voice fit
Why these axes. AI/ML capability is mandated by the assignment AND is the engine that makes the playbook codify (the brief requires AI/ML to be a key value-prop dimension). Voice fit is the operator's #1 make-or-break - one bad draft kills the trial (Katie's verbatim rejection of generic AI: "not suitable"). Together they answer the operator's central question: "is this actually good at writing replies in my voice?"
Where each player sits and why.
  • 1. ChatGPT / Claude — frontier AI capability, generic voice. No playbook access, no Lorne dataset, no learning. Output reads "AI-shaped".
  • 2. Word doc templates — no AI, but the owner wrote them so voice is genuinely theirs. Until they rot.
  • 3. OpenTable / SevenRooms — some AI for matching, none for drafting. They don't reply to high-stakes inquiries at all (Slide 3).
  • 4. VA agencies — human, but a stranger writing in your voice. Brand-damage risk.
  • NightDraft — LLM-native + Lorne 24-thread playbook + 9-template voice library + 5-register sign-off ladder + edit-logging compounding loop. Top-right alone.
  • ★ Segment A ideal (Katie) — voice fit critical, AI/ML invisible to her ("she doesn't care it's AI as long as it works"). Sits inside NightDraft's quadrant.
  • ★ Segment B ideal (Chris/David) — marketing-trained brain wants both. Sits adjacent to NightDraft.
Map 2 · Competitive defence · why competitors can't replicate
Owner control × Self-improvement
Why these axes. Owner control is Filter 2 from Slide 8 (the hard rejection of autosend - Katie verbatim: "I wouldn't want it just kind of sent off"). Self-improvement is the moat - the data flywheel that compounds privately per restaurant, the same Y Combinator / Gary Tan thesis on defensible AI products. Together they answer: "why won't ChatGPT or OpenTable just copy this?"
Where each player sits and why.
  • 1. ChatGPT / Claude — you copy-paste, you decide; max control, but no memory between conversations. No learning loop.
  • 2. Word doc templates — you wrote it, you control it; can't update itself. It rots.
  • 3. OpenTable / SevenRooms — rule-based, you set up rules but updating them is manual.
  • 4. VA agencies — out of your hands; high turnover destroys any institutional learning.
  • 5. Reservations manager hire — your hire, learns over time, but only one head; leaves and the knowledge leaves with them. Plus: GBP 35-45k/yr puts it economically out of reach for indies.
  • NightDraft — no autosend (max control). Edit-logging + Jack & Jill conversational context capture (compounding learning). Top-right alone again.
Strategic claim. NightDraft sits alone in the top-right of both maps because (1) no one combined frontier AI with restaurant-specific vertical depth at indie price points until 2025, and (2) the learning loop compounds privately per restaurant - every edit makes the next draft better, every restaurant's playbook becomes irreplicable. Demand fit + competitive defence, both confirmed.

Why three tiers, not flat pricing
  • Different segments have different WTP. Flat pricing leaves money on the table for groups.
  • Tiering preserves the indie wedge while capturing group economics.
  • Per-venue pricing for groups mirrors hospitality SaaS norms (OpenTable, SevenRooms).
Price discrimination justified by
1

Volume of value

Groups capture more per venue: more inquiries × higher AOV.

2

Implementation depth

Group accounts get cross-concept playbook integration.

3

Self-selection

Indies don't need group features; groups need them.

Price-war prevention (S08 lecture)
  • Don't compete with OpenTable on price (10x larger, always wins on volume).
  • Don't compete with ChatGPT on price (free / GBP 20).
  • Compete on category, not price. "Only sales tool for high-stakes inquiries."
  • Ladder up via segments (indie → group → premium), not discount down.
